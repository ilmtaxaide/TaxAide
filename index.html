A few things people ask before their first visit

Is this really free, no strings attached?

Yes. No fee is charged for preparation or e-filing, and nothing is deducted from a refund. The program is funded by donations and run by volunteers, not a tax-prep business.

Do I need to be a certain age or income level?

No. While many visitors are older adults or moderate-income households, help is open to taxpayers of any age whose return falls within the program's scope — mainly wage income, retirement income, and common credits rather than complex business or investment returns.

What if my return is too complicated for this program?

A volunteer will tell you at intake if your return falls outside what the program can prepare — for example, rental property with depreciation, or a complex small business. In that case they'll explain why and point you toward a paid preparer or the IRS's own guidance.

What to bring to your appointment

A volunteer can only prepare the parts of your return they have paperwork for. Missing a document is the single biggest reason a visit has to be rescheduled — so it's worth five minutes to check this list before you leave the house.

Bring the actual documents, not photos on your phone if you can help it. Volunteers need to read small print on forms like 1099s, and a screen glare makes that harder than it should be.

Property tax relief for homeowners

North Carolina runs a few separate programs that reduce the property tax bill for qualifying homeowners. They're easy to miss because none of them apply automatically — you have to file an application with your county.

Elderly / Disabled Exclusion

Age 65+ or totally & permanently disabled

Excludes the greater of $25,000 or 50% of your home's assessed value from taxation, for homeowners under an income ceiling that's set and updated by the state each year.

Circuit Breaker Tax Deferment

Age 65+ or disabled, owned home 5+ years

Caps your property tax at a percentage of income; the deferred balance becomes a lien and is only due if the home is sold or the owner no longer qualifies.

Disabled Veteran Exclusion

Veterans with a qualifying service-connected disability

Excludes up to $45,000 of assessed value from property tax, regardless of income, for a permanent residence owned by an honorably discharged disabled veteran or their surviving spouse.

Circuit Breaker for Renters

Varies by county pilot programs

Some NC counties separately offer relief tied to rent paid, recognizing that landlords pass property tax through to tenants. Ask your county tax office if one is active locally.

How to apply

1

Get the form

Your county tax office publishes the AV-9 application on its website, or a volunteer can print one for you at your appointment.

2

Gather proof of income

You'll need last year's total household income — Social Security, pensions, and any other income all count toward the limit.

3

File by June 1

Applications are due to your county tax assessor by June 1 each year; once approved, most exclusions renew automatically.

A volunteer can walk through eligibility with you during a regular tax appointment, but the application itself is filed separately with your county tax office — not with your tax return.

Volunteer training & certification

Every preparer here passes an IRS certification exam before touching a real return, and recertifies every single year as tax law changes. You don't need an accounting degree — you need patience and about twenty hours in December and January.

The certification path

1

Self-study modules

Work through IRS Link & Learn training online, at your own pace, covering the returns this program actually handles.

2

Practice returns

Local class sessions walk through realistic sample returns using the same software you'll use at your first real appointment.

3

Certification test

An open-book exam confirms you can apply what you learned — most volunteers pass on the first try after the practice sessions.

Certification levels

You can stop at Basic and still be useful — many of the busiest days need people at every level.

Basic

Entry level

Covers wages, standard deduction, common credits like Earned Income Tax Credit and Child Tax Credit — the majority of returns prepared here.

Advanced

Most volunteers work toward this

Adds retirement income, capital gains, self-employment, and itemized deductions — required to prepare or review most returns unsupervised.

Returning volunteers recertify each year with a shorter refresher course, since the certification only covers one tax year at a time.

Become a volunteer

The program runs entirely on volunteers — there's no professional tax background required for most roles, and training is provided before the season starts.

Roles that need people

Tax preparer

  • Completes IRS certification each fall
  • Meets one-on-one with taxpayers
  • ~1 shift per week, Jan–Apr

Client facilitator

  • Greets visitors and checks appointments
  • Confirms documents before intake
  • No tax certification required

Quality reviewer

  • Requires Advanced certification
  • Double-checks every return before filing
  • A good fit for detail-oriented volunteers
